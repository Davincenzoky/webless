Historical Antecedents in which Social Considerations Changed the Course of Science and Technology
a. In the World
1. Ancient Times: Mesoamerican Period (Inca, Aztec, and Mayan Civilizations)
Social Considerations:
Religious beliefs, agricultural demands, and social hierarchies drove technological innovation.
Examples of Work:
Inca:
Terrace farming (to maximize agriculture on mountain slopes).
Quipu (a knotted string system used for record-keeping and communication — a precursor to writing for administrative purposes).
Aztec:
Chinampas ("floating gardens") - artificial islands used for farming to meet the growing food demands of dense urban populations like Tenochtitlán.
Calendar Stone (Aztec Sun Stone) - an astronomical and religious artifact that aligned religious ceremonies with astronomical events.
Mayan:
Maya Calendar Systems (e.g., Tzolk'in and Haab') - advanced understanding of astronomy influenced by religious rituals.
Pyramids and temples (like Chichen Itza) - architectural feats aligned with astronomical observations for religious p
2. Middle Ages (5th–15th Century Europe)
Social Considerations:
Feudalism, religious authority (especially the Catholic Church), and the need for survival during war and plague strongly influenced science and technology.
Examples of Work:
Watermills and windmills - innovations to enhance food production and reduce manual labor on feudal estates.
Gutenberg Printing Press (c. 1440) - revolutionized access to information, breaking the Church's monopoly over written texts and promoting wider education.
Islamic Golden Age (8th–14th century) - advancements in mathematics (Algebra by Al-Khwarizmi), medicine (Avicenna’s The Canon of Medicine), and optics (Ibn al-Haytham), all driven by the Islamic emphasis on knowledge as a religious duty.
3. Modern Ages (16th Century to Present)
Social Considerations:
Industrialization, democratization, world wars, and global health crises accelerated technological progress.
Examples of Work:
Industrial Revolution Technologies (18th-19th century):
Steam Engine by James Watt - mass urban migration and need for efficient transport and production.
Spinning Jenny (textile industry) - driven by the demand for mass clothing production.
World War I and II Technologies:
Radar, Penicillin, and Nuclear Technology - military needs drove rapid scientific advancements.
Digital Revolution:
Internet (ARPANET, 1969) - initially military-focused (U.S. Department of Defense) but transformed global communication and society.
COVID-19 Vaccines (Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna mRNA vaccines) - pandemic urgency accelerated medical innovation.

b. In the Philippines
Social Considerations:
Colonization, indigenous practices, liberation movements, and modern nation-building significantly shaped science and technology.
Examples of Work:
Pre-colonial Technology:
Banaue Rice Terraces - engineered by the Ifugao people over 2,000 years ago; sophisticated water irrigation and agricultural knowledge driven by food sustainability needs.
Spanish Colonization Period:
Galleon Shipbuilding (Manila-Acapulco Galleon Trade) - driven by trade needs; Filipinos were skilled in ship construction.
Establishment of hospitals and schools (e.g., San Juan de Dios Hospital, founded 1596) - blending Spanish health practices with native healing traditions.
American Period (early 20th century):
Introduction of public education system - spread of science literacy through Thomasites (American teachers).
Philippine General Hospital (PGH) founded in 1907 - advancement of Western medical practices in the country.
Modern Period:
Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) - developed for disaster preparedness (especially typhoons and earthquakes).
Rice Research at IRRI (International Rice Research Institute, Los Baños) - Green Revolution led to improved rice varieties like IR8 ("Miracle Rice") to address food security.

Intellectual Revolutions that Defined Society
a. Copernican Revolution
Overview:
Initiated by Nicolaus Copernicus in the 16th century.
Proposed the heliocentric model (Sun at the center) over the long-accepted geocentric model (Earth at the center).
Impact on Society:
Challenged the authority of the Church and ancient scholars (like Aristotle and Ptolemy).
Marked the beginning of the Scientific Revolution.
Changed humanity’s self-perception — Earth was no longer the center of the universe.
Example of Work:
"De Revolutionibus Orbium Coelestium" (On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres) by Copernicus (1543)
b. Darwinian Revolution
Overview:
Led by Charles Darwin in the 19th century.
Introduced the theory of evolution by natural selection.
Impact on Society:
Shattered traditional religious beliefs about creation.
Influenced biology, anthropology, and even social sciences (e.g., social Darwinism, although controversially).
Example of Work:
"On the Origin of Species" by Charles Darwin (1859).
c. Freudian Revolution
Overview:
Initiated by Sigmund Freud in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
Developed psychoanalysis and emphasized the role of the unconscious mind in shaping behavior.
Impact on Society:
Changed the way society understood human behavior, sexuality, childhood development, and mental illness.
Influenced literature, art, and modern psychology.
Example of Work:
"The Interpretation of Dreams" by Sigmund Freud (1900).
d. Information Revolution
Overview:
Refers to the late 20th and 21st-century shift caused by advances in information and communication technology (ICT).
Impact on Society:
Transformed economies into information-based economies.
Changed communication, education, business, and governance (e.g., e-governance, online banking, remote work).
Example of Work:
Development of the Internet (ARPANET in 1969 to World Wide Web in 1991 by Tim Berners-Lee).
Invention of the personal computer (e.g., Apple II, 1977).
e. Mesoamerican Intellectual Contributions
Overview:
Includes the knowledge systems of Maya, Aztec, and Inca civilizations.
Impact on Society:
Advanced understanding of astronomy, mathematics, agriculture, and architecture.
Societies were deeply scientific and religious at the same time.
Example of Work:
Mayan Calendar Systems (Long Count Calendar).
Aztec Medicine and Surgery -herbal remedies, skull surgeries (trepanation).

f. Asian Intellectual Contributions
Overview:
Intellectual achievements from China, India, Japan, and other Asian civilizations.
Impact on Society:
Major contributions in philosophy, mathematics, medicine, and technology.
Spread of philosophies like Confucianism, Buddhism, Hinduism, and Taoism shaped not just governments but social norms.
Example of Work:
Invention of Paper (Cai Lun, China, 2nd century CE).
Decimal Number System and Zero (India - Aryabhata and Brahmagupta).

g. Middle Eastern Intellectual Contributions
Overview:
Mainly from the Islamic Golden Age (8th to 14th centuries).
Impact on Society:
Major advancements in science, medicine, astronomy, mathematics, and philosophy preserved and built upon Greek, Persian, and Indian knowledge.
Example of Work:
"The Book of Healing" and "The Canon of Medicine" by Avicenna (Ibn Sina).
Development of Algebra by Al-Khwarizmi.

h. African Intellectual Contributions
Overview:
Includes the intellectual systems of ancient African civilizations (e.g., Egypt, Mali Empire, Great Zimbabwe).
Impact on Society:
Pioneered in architecture, astronomy, mathematics, and metallurgy.
Built complex social structures based on oral traditions and practical sciences.
Example of Work:
Great Library of Alexandria (Egypt — although multicultural, much African intellectual contribution).
Mathematical systems in ancient Egypt (pyramids aligned with astronomical points).
Timbuktu Manuscripts (Mali) — preserved works on astronomy, law, and medicine.


Science and Technology and Nation Building
a. The Philippine Government S&T Agenda
Overview:
The Philippine government recognizes that science, technology, and innovation are critical for national development, economic competitiveness, and social progress.
Key Programs and Strategies:
DOST (Department of Science and Technology) S&T Agenda:
Strengthen research and development (R&D) in key areas: health, agriculture, industry, energy, and emerging technologies (AI, nanotech, space tech).
Science for the People Program — bringing R&D outputs to marginalized communities.
Harmonized National Research and Development Agenda (HNRDA) — align research efforts to national priorities (2017-2022, updated for 2023+).
Philippine Development Plan (PDP) 2023-2028:
Focuses on building resilient infrastructure, digitalization, innovation-driven industries, and enhancing climate resilience through science.
Balik Scientist Program:
Encourage Filipino scientists abroad to return and contribute to national scientific and technological development.

b. Major Development Programs and Personalities in S&T in the Philippines
Major Development Programs:
PAGASA Modernization Program:
Improved disaster forecasting and management (especially for typhoons and earthquakes).
Advanced Science and Technology Institute (ASTI):
Leading projects like Diwata-1 (the first Philippine microsatellite) and PhilSA (Philippine Space Agency).
OneLab Network:
Integrated laboratory testing services to support industries (especially SMEs).
SETUP (Small Enterprise Technology Upgrading Program):
Helped micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) adopt technological innovations.

c. Science Education in the Philippines
Overview:
Education has been a primary channel for promoting scientific thinking and technological literacy.
Key Developments:
Special Science High Schools:
Philippine Science High School (PSHS) System - premier institution developing future scientists.
Curriculum Enhancement:
K-12 Science Curriculum: Emphasis on inquiry-based learning, STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Mathematics) integration.
Scholarship Programs:
DOST-SEI Scholarships: Encourage students to pursue careers in science and engineering fields.
Higher Education Initiatives:
Centers of Excellence (COEs) in science and engineering designated by CHED (Commission on Higher Education).
Challenges:
Unequal access between rural and urban areas.
Need for more research funding and infrastructure support.
d. Selected Indigenous Science and Technologies
Overview:
Before colonization, Filipinos developed native technologies based on environmental adaptation, cultural needs, and community cooperation.
Examples of Indigenous Technologies:
Banaue Rice Terraces (Ifugao):
Sophisticated irrigation and farming techniques adapted to mountainous terrains.
Traditional Shipbuilding (Balangay boats):
Early seafaring technology enabling trade and migration across Southeast Asia.
Herbal Medicine Practices:
Use of plants like lagundi, sambong, and tawa-tawa for healing purposes.
Bamboo and Rattan Engineering:
Crafting houses, furniture, and tools using sustainable native materials.
T'nalak Weaving (T'boli people):
Traditional fiber weaving from abaca; integration of mathematical patterning and cultural storytelling.
