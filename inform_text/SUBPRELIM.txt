Data Storage and Retrieval Systems 
“Where do your photos, school files, and social media posts actually live and how do you find them instantly?”
What Are Data Storage and Retrieval Systems?
Data storage and retrieval systems are technologies and methods used to store data securely and retrieve it efficiently when needed.
They ensure:
Data is available
Data is accurate
Data is protected

Databases and Data Warehouses
“Why can banks retrieve your transaction history in seconds—even from years ago?”

What is a Database?
A database is an organized collection of structured data stored electronically and managed by a Database Management System (DBMS).
Examples of database use:
Student records
Bank accounts
Online shopping systems
Common DBMS: MySQL, Oracle, PostgreSQL, SQL Server
What is a Data Warehouse?
A data warehouse stores large amounts of historical data from multiple sources for analysis and reporting, not daily transactions.
Database vs. Data Warehouse

Hard drives

Cloud storage allows data to be stored on remote servers and accessed over the internet.
Examples: Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, iCloud

Privacy Laws (GDPR, HIPAA, and Others)
“Why do websites ask you to accept cookies?”

- Cookies are files created by websites you visit. By saving information about your visit, they make your online experience easier. For example, sites can keep you signed in, remember your site preferences, and give you locally relevant content.

-Cache refers to a storage layer that saves copies of web resources, such as HTML pages, images, JavaScript, and CSS files, to improve website performance. By storing these resources locally, the cache reduces the time it takes to load frequently visited pages and decreases the amount of data that needs to be retrieved from the server. This means that upon revisiting a site, the browser can quickly load these resources from the cache rather than downloading them again.
